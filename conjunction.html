Cyan is you, red the target, and the dashed white line the orbit a planned burn would give you. A ring marks a closest approach.

In the close-in view, the target is at the center, the direction it travels is to the right, and up is away from Earth. The dashed shape is how close it would let you come, from each direction, before noticing you, colored by what would notice you there: cameras, infrared, radar, or the jammer being heard. The cyan dotted ring is your strike range.

Keys: arrows plan a burn, Enter burns, Space pauses, + and − set the warp, 1–4 skip ahead, F fires, J jams, G switches engines, V switches views.

Conjunction

You are an autonomous interceptor in low Earth orbit. Your orders: destroy one satellite, and don't let it see you coming.

    Paused

    How to play

    Orbits

    Everything here obeys orbital mechanics. A prograde burn, along your direction of travel, raises the far side of your orbit, and a higher orbit is a slower one. So to catch a target ahead of you in the same orbit, burn retrograde: you drop into a lower, faster orbit and gain on it every lap. To reach a higher target, burn prograde when it is a little ahead of you, and coast half an orbit up to it (a Hohmann transfer).

    Planning a burn

    Drag the burn pad, or use the + and − buttons. Right is prograde, left retrograde, up away from Earth, down toward it. The dashed line is the orbit the burn would give you, and Planned is the closest it would bring you to the target in the next few orbits. Nothing happens until you press Burn, and the burn is made at that moment, so you can leave a burn planned, let time run, and watch for the moment it lines up.

    The main engine has plenty of propellant but a bright plume. The cold-gas thrusters are nearly invisible, but carry little. Tap Main or Cold gas to choose.

    Time

    Use + and − to speed time up, to 1000 times. Apo, Peri, Closest and Shadow skip ahead and pause. Time slows down by itself when the target is near, and, with a burn planned, as its window opens; it pauses at the window's best moment.

    Staying hidden

    The target's suspicion rises while it can sense you, and slowly falls when it can't. In the close-in view, a dashed shape shows how close you could come, from each direction, before it noticed, and its color says which sensor would notice you first: yellow for cameras, red for infrared, violet for radar, pink for your jammer being heard. Switch the jammer on and the violet shrinks, but pink can take its place.

    • Sunlight: its cameras see you when you are lit. Earth's shadow hides you, and so does the Sun's glare, if you come at it with the Sun behind you.
    • The Earth behind you: against the Earth below, you are much harder to pick out than against black space.
    • Heat: you glow faintly in infrared all the time.
    • Plumes: main-engine burns near the target are bright.
    • Radar: some targets have radar, which works in the dark but only up close. Your jammer cuts its range to a quarter, but it drains the battery, and the target can hear the jamming within about 3 km.

    At 40% the target is suspicious and looks harder. At 100% it is alerted for good: it dodges when you come near, and its decoys are ready.

    Power

    Your solar panels charge the battery in sunlight. In Earth's shadow you run on it. If it goes flat you can't burn, jam or fire until it recovers.

    Striking

    Fire an interceptor from within 5 km. It needs time to get there, and if the target reacts first, its decoys draw the shot off. An unaware target takes 20 seconds to react, a suspicious one 12, an alerted one 5. Get close, or fire while closing fast. A target with no decoys left can't stop a shot.

    Rating

    One star for destroying the target, one for never alerting it, and one for using no more propellant than the mission's par.
